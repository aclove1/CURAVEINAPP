## 1. Executive Summary

The corridor has exactly the inventory this use case needs, but it clusters in **inner Dallas, not North Dallas or Plano**. The three strongest candidates are all true storefront live/work, not loft-styled apartments:

1. **Trinity Lofts, 1430 Dragon St (Design District)** — purpose-built dual-story live/work: ground-floor retail/gallery with its own storefront entrance, customer parking, and a garage, plus a 1BR above. Explicitly marketed for galleries and creative studios. The true storefront suites run $3,600–$5,032/mo (over the hard cap but inside the documented exception); residential split-level lofts have listed at $2,350–$2,450 for 1,857–2,153 sqft.
2. **Bishop Arts Live/Work Lofts #422 (Oak Cliff)** — first-floor units with a street-facing retail entrance, a rear resident door, and **zoning that explicitly permits running a small business**. From $1,350/mo. Catch: most units are ~550 sqft, so you must secure one of the larger ~1,000–1,150 sqft units.
3. **Expo Park Lofts — Paper Mill / B.F. Goodrich / GMC Building (Exposition Park)** — historic industrial conversions self-described as the "most unique live/work space in Dallas," already home to art galleries and photography studios. Lowest friction for client visits and oversized work; pricing needs confirmation.

North Dallas (Addison) and Plano deliver **loft styling and arts-district adjacency but not genuine live/work tolerance** at this budget; treat them as residence-near-the-scene fallbacks, not studio/gallery solutions. Best authentic-loft-with-ceiling-evidence backup is the **Interurban Building** (confirmed 11–15 ft ceilings, downtown).

## 5. Live/Work Feasibility Analysis (Step 5)

### Dallas zoning reality
- **Legally permitted:** Mixed-use districts (Ch. 51A-4.125, MU-1/MU-2/MU-3 allow residential + office/retail/personal-service in one structure) and **Planned Development (PD) districts** that explicitly authorize ground-floor commercial under residential. Dallas adopted **live/work unit definitions via a 2018 code amendment** (a "live unit" is a dwelling accessory to a permitted nonresidential use; one per lot; cannot be sold separately). Purpose-built live/work in the **Design District (Trinity Lofts)** and **Bishop Arts (units zoned for small business)** falls here.
- **Technically not allowed but tolerated:** Residential leases in arts-friendly conversions — **Adam Hats, Deep Ellum Lofts, South Side on Lamar, Interurban, Davis**. You can paint and host occasional visitors; running an advertised retail gallery with signage and steady foot traffic exceeds the residential lease and is *tolerated until it isn't* (noise/parking/odor complaints are the usual trigger).
- **Enforced against / dead ends:** **Continental Gin Building** — converted away from residential to offices; you legally cannot reside there. Condo buildings with restrictive HOAs (**SoCo Urban Lofts**) enforce against commercial activity. Conventional managed communities (**MAA/Cortland/AMLI Addison, CityLine luxury, Trend Design District, Southside Flats**) bar commercial/client use by standard lease.

### Plano zoning reality
- **Permitted-in-district:** Plano allows live/work in **Central/Downtown Business districts, Special-Purpose/PD districts, and Mixed-Use districts**, defining live/work as a dwelling whose work component is limited to office/artist's-workshop/studio/personal-service uses, **with the work component at street level.** The Downtown Plano Arts District (15th St) is the venue.
- **Tolerated:** New-construction apartments in the arts district (**@15th**) — arts-adjacent residence, work use tolerated case-by-case.
- **Enforced against:** Legacy West / Legacy East / Shops at Legacy (corporate MU; ground-floor retail is commercial-only leasing, residential is standard luxury) and all standard suburban garden apartments.

### Buildings known for creative tenants (verified signal)
Trinity Lofts (galleries/creative studios), Expo Park (Paper Mill / B.F. Goodrich / GMC — active galleries + photo studios), Bishop Arts live/work (creatives + small businesses), South Side on Lamar (artist quarters + rotating gallery), Adam Hats & Deep Ellum Lofts (Deep Ellum arts scene).

### Buildings where you will get shut down
- **Continental Gin Building** — no residential use permitted (offices only).
- **SoCo Urban Lofts** and other for-sale **condo** lofts — HOA enforces against commercial activity/signage/foot traffic.
- **MAA Addison Circle, Cortland Addison, AMLI Addison, CityLine luxury communities, Trend Design District, Southside Flats** — conventional residential leases prohibit running a business with client visits.

1. Confirm rent is all-in ≤ $3,500 (or document the storefront exception ≤ $3,800).
2. Get the ceiling height in writing or by photo (≥10 ft min, ≥12 ft preferred).
3. Get the **client-visit / signage / commercial-use** policy in the lease or HOA docs — do not rely on "tolerated."
